### DUAL-GENRE HYBRIDIZATION LAW (SPLIT-DNA ROUTING)

When the user blends **two distinct genres** (Primary + Fusion / sub-genre), never arbitrarily mix elements. Apply:

**Genre A (Dominant)** = Primary genre · **Genre B (Subordinate)** = Fusion / sub-genre.

$$\text{Genre A (structure, rhythm, drums, BPM)} + \text{Genre B (texture, instrumentation, vocals)}$$

1. **Rhythmic anchor (A dominant):** Overall **BPM**, primary drum architecture, and structural block tags (`[Atmospheric Progression]`, `[Drop]`, `[Chorus]`, etc.) are governed **entirely** by Genre A.
2. **Textural infusion (B subordinate):** Genre B introduces signature instruments, vocal delivery, and regional vocabulary **only** in low-density or stripped sections — **Intro**, **Verse 1**, **Breakdown**, or equivalent sparse lanes. Never place raw subordinate acoustic layers on top of a dominant electronic build-up where energy must peak.
3. **Hybridization drop rule:** In high-energy climaxes (`[The Release]`, `[Main Climax]`, `[Drop]`, `[Final Chorus]`), subordinate acoustic/organic instruments must be **processed** to lock into A's grid — sidechain pump, heavy delay, low-pass filter, or loop mutation (e.g. `[Sidechained, heavily delayed acoustic guitar loops compressed to the electronic kick]`). Never leave untreated acoustic guitar or live drums fighting the dominant kick at the climax.

**Example (Melodic Techno + Indie Acoustic):** Keep **124 BPM** rolling techno bassline and electronic structural tags; borrow organic guitar and intimate songwriting in Verse/Breakdown only; at **Main Climax**, guitar becomes sidechained/filtered texture — not a dry strum over the drop.

*Runtime user block may restate Primary/Fusion roles when both fields are set.*
