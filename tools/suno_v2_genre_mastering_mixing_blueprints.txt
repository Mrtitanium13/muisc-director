### 1. Hip-Hop / Boom Bap / Trap / Drill

**Mixing architecture:** Bottom-up balancing; 808 and kick established first to define headroom. Parallel compression on drums for "knock" while maintaining transients.

**Instrumentation:** SP-1200 grit on drum chops. 808s with portamento slides and distortion at the clip threshold.

**Mastering:** −7 to −9 LUFS (Trap) / −10 to −12 LUFS (Boom Bap). −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Dynamic EQ to control 808 fundamental peaks.

### 2. EDM / House / Techno / Hardstyle

**Mixing architecture:** Aggressive sidechain ducking on bass/synths triggered by kick. Mono-compatible low end (sub-200 Hz) with ultra-wide Haas effect on leads.

**Instrumentation:** OTT (upward/downward) compression on synths. Hard-limited festival kicks with sharp transient punch.

**Mastering:** −6 to −8 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Hard clipping before the final limiter to shave transients.

### 3. R&B / Neo-Soul / Bedroom R&B

**Mixing architecture:** Vocal intimacy and "breath" using warm plate reverbs. LCR panning for organic instrument separation.

**Instrumentation:** Rhodes/piano with sustain-pedal wash and soft vibrato. Fretless bass with smooth harmonic bloom.

**Mastering:** −12 to −14 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Analog tape emulation for high-end roll-off and harmonic glue.

### 4. Rock / Metal / Punk

**Mixing architecture:** Mid-forward EQ priority; subtractive EQ around 3 kHz to clear space for vocals. SSL-style bus compression (~2:1) for cohesive glue.

**Instrumentation:** Drum room mic saturation for physical space. Guttural-friendly vowel shaping on vocals.

**Mastering:** −8 to −10 LUFS (Metal) / ~−12 LUFS (Indie). −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Preserve snare snap through transparent limiting.

### 5. Praise & Worship / Gospel

**Mixing architecture:** Wide congregational mix with long natural reverb tails. Intimate verses swelling into anthemic, multi-layered choruses.

**Instrumentation:** Clean chorus electric guitars and SATB choir harmonies. B3 organ with natural tonewheel grit.

**Mastering:** −12 to −14 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Preserve high dynamic range for emotional builds.

### 6. Mandopop / K-Pop / J-Pop

**Mixing architecture:** Polished commercial pop prioritizing vocal clarity and melodic presence. Cinematic strings blended with pentatonic leads.

**Instrumentation:** Clean programmed drums with woodblock or gong accents. Traditional textures (erhu, guzheng, etc.) woven into Western pop structures.

**Mastering:** −8 to −10 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Bright high-frequency excitement without harsh sibilance.

### 7. Amapiano / Afrobeats

**Mixing architecture:** Deep log-drum grooves locked to round sub-bass. Shaker-heavy polyrhythmic percussion on the 16th-note grid.

**Instrumentation:** Jazz piano with major-ninth chords and bright attack. Wide stereo room reverb on female vocal hooks.

**Mastering:** −9 to −11 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Deep low-end warmth with a transparent top end.

### 8. Vinahouse

**Mixing architecture:** Bouncy four-on-the-floor kick with sharp offbeat accents. Fast 16th hi-hats and percussive cymbal rushes.

**Instrumentation:** Đàn tranh zither or synthesized flute hooks. Pentatonic vocal chops with controlled sibilance and wide panning.

**Mastering:** −7 to −9 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Maximum bright energy and hard-hitting drop dynamics.

### 9. Reggae / Dancehall

**Mixing architecture:** Riddim focus — bass and drum interplay as the primary structure. Dub-style delay throws on snare and vocals.

**Instrumentation:** Bubble organ and muted guitar skanks. Emphasis on the third-beat kick/snare feel.

**Mastering:** −10 to −12 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. Warm, analog-style saturation.

### 10. Jazz / Blues / Cinematic

**Mixing architecture:** Minimal compression to preserve natural dynamics. Large hall or chamber reverb for cinematic depth.

**Instrumentation:** Solo violin or cello with expressive vibrato. Acoustic rhythm sections with soft rim-clicks.

**Mastering:** −14 to −16 LUFS. −1.0 dBTP ceiling. High fidelity; avoid clipping to preserve timbre.
